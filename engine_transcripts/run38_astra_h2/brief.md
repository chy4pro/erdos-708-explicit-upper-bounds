# PROOF CAMPAIGN — Erdős 708: the hinge inequality at threshold 2 (GPT-6 Astra; no wall-clock cap; SINGLE AGENT — do not spawn sub-agents, work sequentially; this is a hard budget constraint, sub-agents multiply the cost about tenfold)

## The statement
An atom system is a FINITELY SUPPORTED family of weights α_{p,j} ≥ 0 on prime powers p^j (j ≥ 1) with Σ_j α_{p,j} ≤ 1 for every prime p; S(n) = Σ_{p,j} α_{p,j}[p^j | n]. For m ≥ 1 and a window I = {x+1,…,x+m},
   (H_c)   Σ_{k=1}^{m} (S(k) − c)^+ ≤ Σ_{b∈I} (S(b) − 1)^+ .
TARGET T1: prove (H_2). TARGET T2 (equal rank): refute it, with an explicit atom system, m and window in exact arithmetic. TARGET T3 (fallback, only after both freeze): prove (H_c) for the smallest c you can below 56501/6400 = 8.8283.

## Why it is worth a campaign
A kernel-checked reduction gives g(n) ≤ ⌈cn⌉ + 2n from (H_c), where g is the Erdős–Surányi function of Erdős problem 708 ($100, open since 1959). Our published bound is g(n) ≤ 12n, kernel-verified in Lean, from (H_{97/10}); (H_{56501/6400}) is refereed and gives 11n. (H_2) would give g(n) ≤ 4n. It is also the exact floor of this route: (H_c) is FALSE for every c < 2 (take r primes in [M, √2·M) with M > 2, all weights 1, m = p_{r−1}p_r, and the window centred at their product: the left side is C(r,2)(2 − c), the right side is r − 1, so it fails once c < 2 − 2/r, and such prime clusters exist for every r because Σ 1/p diverges). So c = 2 is the best possible threshold and (H_2) is the last thing this route can give.

## Everything already proved (verify each before building on it; all refereed, several kernel-checked)
R1 PRIMORIAL TRUNCATION. For an integer d ≥ 0 and Q_d = p_1⋯p_d the d-th primorial (Q_2 = 6, Q_3 = 30, Q_4 = 210), let S_0 keep exactly the atoms p^j with p^j·Q_d ≤ m. Then for every k ≤ m and every real c ≥ d, (S(k) − c)^+ = (S_0(k) − c)^+, so the left side is unchanged, and S_0 ≤ S makes the right side no larger. Q_d is optimal. AT c = 2 ONE MAY THEREFORE ASSUME EVERY ATOM IS AT MOST m/6. Proof: an omitted atom q with qQ_d > m dividing k ≤ m gives k = qt with t < Q_d, so ω(t) ≤ d−1; q is a prime power so ω(k) ≤ d; the per-prime capacity gives S(k) ≤ ω(k) ≤ d ≤ c.
R2 DENSE BRANCH. If the m/6-truncated system has mean H = Σ α_{p,j}/p^j at least h_0 = (18 − √30)/7 = 1.78896… then (H_2) holds. (Rescale V = (h_0/H)S_0, use ⌊m/q⌋ ≥ (6/7)(m/q) for q ≤ m/6, the moment bound Σ_{k≤m} e_r((f_p(k))_p) ≤ mH^r/r! and (Σu_i − 2)^+ ≤ e_2(u)/3, then the affine certificate S − 1. NOTE the rescaling: f(h) = (6/7)h − h²/6 − 1 is a downward parabola, positive only on [h_0, (18+√30)/7], so one must apply the lemma with the parameter h = h_0 and V ≤ S_0, not substitute h = H.) Likewise μ − H²/6 ≥ 1 implies (H_2), where μ = (1/m)Σ_{k≤m} S(k); under the hypotheses below τ = 1 is the optimal choice in that family.
R3 FOUR PRIMES. If at most four primes are active, (H_2) holds. Certificate: G = (1/3) Σ_{i<j} (f_i + f_j − 1)^+ with f_i the contribution of the i-th prime, padded to four; (S − 2)^+ ≤ G ≤ (S − 1)^+ pointwise (lower: each f_i lies in three of the six pairs so Σ ≥ 3S − 6; upper: the six pairs split into three perfect matchings and (a−1)^+ + (b−1)^+ ≤ (a+b−1)^+). Each pair term expands into NONNEGATIVE divisor increments β_{a,b} = φ(A_a+B_b) − φ(A_{a−1}+B_b) − φ(A_a+B_{b−1}) + φ(A_{a−1}+B_{b−1}) ≥ 0 with φ(u) = (u−1)^+ and A, B the cumulative weights, because u ↦ φ(u+δ) − φ(u) is nondecreasing; telescoping gives g_{pq}(n) = Σ_{a,b≥1} β_{a,b}[p^a q^b | n], and #{b ∈ I : d | b} ≥ ⌊m/d⌋ finishes.
R4 FIVE PRIMES, ONE MULTILEVEL. If at most five primes are active and at most ONE of them carries more than one nonzero atom, (H_2) holds. Method (elimination): write S = T + f with T on the four single-atom primes and f on the fifth; use the exact layer identity (T + f − a)^+ − (T − a)^+ = ∫_0^1 [f > t][T > a − t] dt, valid for f ∈ [0,1] and any real a and T (the per-prime capacity is the only hypothesis); for each t > 0 the indicator [f(n) > t] is either identically zero or [p^{j(t)} | n] with j(t) = min{j : A_j > t} ≥ 1, because a prime's contribution is a cumulative step function of v_p(n); then a Boolean antichain bound charges the layer count to ⌊m/(d Q(t))⌋.
R5 BOOLEAN ANTICHAIN BOUND. For pairwise coprime 2 ≤ a < b < c < d and an integer Q ≥ 2, the inclusion-minimal nonempty A ⊆ {a,b,c,d} with (∏A)/max A ≥ Q satisfy Q Σ_A (∏A)^{-1} ≤ 1; the maximum is 101/105 at ({2,3,5,7}, Q = 2), a margin under 4%. Pairwise coprimality is essential: (2,3,4,5) with Q = 2 gives 71/60 > 1.
R6 ANY NUMBER OF PRIMES UNDER A CAP. For every N ≥ 2, if at most N primes are active and each has total weight at most λ = 2/(N−1), then (H_2) holds. Certificate G(f) = Σ_{r<N} max(0, min(λ/N, min_i (f_i − λ((r+i) mod N)/N))) = λ·P(Z_i ≤ f_i ∀i) with Z_i = λ{U + i/N} and U uniform; union bound for the lower hinge, a branch-length computation for the upper. The cap is load-bearing: at N = 6 with six weights 1/2 > 2/5 the certificate drops below the lower hinge. At five equal half-weights it has value exactly 1/2, where the pair certificate of R3 collapses to 0.
R7 THE STRUCTURAL OBSTRUCTION — READ THIS BEFORE DESIGNING ANY CERTIFICATE. There is NO pointwise nonnegative certificate at six primes: no G(f) = Σ_t c_t ∏_{i : t_i > 0}[f_i ≥ t_i] with all c_t ≥ 0 on {0,1/2,1}^6 satisfies (Σf_i − 2)^+ ≤ G ≤ (Σf_i − 1)^+ everywhere. (The upper bound kills the constant, singleton and (1/2,1/2)-pair coefficients; then P + T + H ≤ 5 at the all-ones state and 4P + T ≥ 20 from the twenty states with three ones force T = H = 0; at the all-half state G = 0 < 1. An exact rational Farkas certificate of infeasibility exists.) Equivalently, for the six-prime system with atoms 1/2 at p_i and 1/2 at p_i², no nonnegative divisor certificate works pointwise. A pointwise nonnegative certificate DOES exist at five primes on {0,1/2,1}^5, so the obstruction begins exactly at six. CONSEQUENCE: beyond five primes a certificate must either be SIGNED (as in the published Sections 14–18, where the window value is estimated with positive moduli counted from below by ⌊m/d⌋ and negative moduli from above by m/d + 1, which forces every negative modulus to be at most m), or it must dominate the left side only AFTER SUMMATION over [1,m] rather than pointwise. The second mechanism is proved to work: the certificate of R4 is dominated by (S − 1)^+ pointwise but dominates (S − 2)^+ only after summation, and indeed fails to dominate it pointwise at an explicit point. (CORRECTION, 14:4x: an earlier version of this sentence had the first relation the wrong way round. Astra caught this.)
R8 THE EXACT REMAINING CASE. (H_2) for all atom systems is equivalent to (H_2) restricted to systems that are m/6-truncated, have at least five active primes, satisfy m ≥ 66, have positive left side, mean H < h_0, and μ − H²/6 < 1. (CORRECTION, 14:4x: an earlier version of this line also required at least two multilevel primes and a prime of total weight above 1/2. Those two conditions follow from R4 and R6 only when there are AT MOST five active primes; they are NOT part of the general remaining case. Astra caught this.) This region is non-empty (an explicit member: m = 210 with atoms 2, 4, 3, 13, 19, 23, 29 of weights 2/3, 1/3, 1, 1, 1, 2/3, 1).
R9 A CONDITIONAL BRIDGE, OPEN. For four distinct primes P and an integer Q ≥ 2, let A_P(Q) be the coordinatewise-minimal exponent vectors e with D(e)/max_i p_i^{e_i} ≥ Q, D(e) = ∏ p_i^{e_i}. The statement K_4: Q Σ_{e ∈ A_P(Q)} D(e)^{-1} ≤ 1 for every P and Q would extend R4 to five UNRESTRICTED primes. It is open; verified for all 4-subsets of the primes below 60 with Q ≤ 400 and for {2,3,5,7} with Q ≤ 4000, with global maximum 101/105. Two structural warnings: it is not monotone in the primes (K_{2,5,7,11}(3) = 111/220 < 236/385 = K_{3,5,7,11}(3)), and the maximising Q is NOT always 2 (K_{2,5,7,11}(4) = 37/55 > 213/385), which kills the natural "reduce to Q = 2 and apply R5" strategy. Even granted, it would not iterate: the five-prime analogue is false, 2 e_2(1/2,1/3,1/5,1/7,1/11) = 194/165 > 1.

## Dead routes — do not re-derive
Pointwise nonnegative certificates in general (refuted on reflected windows (m!−m, m!]; R3 and R6 survive only because of their special structure, and R7 says the whole class dies at six primes). Two-sided interval counts alone (projective planes). Bernoulli rounding and threshold averaging. Any reduction refuted on Hensley–Richards windows, where an interval can contain more integers free of small primes than [1,m] does — test every intermediate statement on such windows and on CRT dense windows at 10^4–10^6 BEFORE using it. Prefix-to-window injections. Exhaustive exact searches have found no counterexample: all weights in {0,1/2,1} on {2,3,5,7} and on {2,3,5,7,11} over every m and every window (1.3·10^9 instances); all weights in {0,1/6,…,1} on {2,3,5,7} likewise (1.06·10^8); and 1.9·10^8 window instances on five to eight primes.
